## Narration script (read this to re-record in your own voice)

1. **RACE SEASON TRAINING PLAN**: Race season is here. This is a four week, in-season training plan built for hare scramble and G N C C style off-road racing. Strength, squats, balance, grip, and running, all built around a weekend race.

2. **THE GOAL**: The goal is simple. Hold attack position for two to three hours without fading. Strong legs mean less arm pump, because you grip the bike with your knees, not your hands. Build an engine for climbs and woods sections, sharpen balance for roots and off-camber, and most important, show up fresh on race day. In season, we train to race, not to get sore.

3. **YOUR WEEK**: Here is your week. Monday is recovery. Tuesday is Strength A, squats, legs, grip, and balance. Wednesday is your engine run with race-pace intervals. Thursday is Strength B, upper body, pulling, and core. Friday is an easy run. Saturday is a light ride, or rest and travel. Sunday is race day. If you race Saturday, shift everything one day earlier.

4. **HOW THE 4 WEEKS PROGRESS**: Here is how the month progresses. Week one, build, at an effort of seven out of ten. Week two, add a set. Week three is the peak, heavier weight with fewer reps. Week four is the taper, cut the volume in half so you are sharp for the biggest race of the block.

5. **PICKING YOUR WEIGHTS**: How to pick your weights. We use R P E, rate of perceived exertion. Seven means you could do three more reps. Eight means two more. Never grind to failure in season. If you are new to lifting, start with a thirty five to fifty five pound dumbbell goblet squat. If you back squat, start around the bar plus half your bodyweight and work up. If the bar slows down or your form breaks, drop the weight ten percent.

6. **WARM-UP  (EVERY SESSION, 8 MIN)**: Every session starts with this eight minute warm-up. Two minutes of jump rope or easy bike. Ten bodyweight squats and ten walking lunges. Leg swings front to back and side to side. Arm circles and band pull-aparts. Then five slow inchworms into a push-up.

7. **STRENGTH A**: Strength A. Tuesday. Legs, squats, grip, and balance. About forty five to fifty minutes.

8. **BACK SQUAT or GOBLET SQUAT**: A one. The back squat, or goblet squat. Week one, three sets of six. Week two, four sets of five. Week three, three sets of four, heavier. Week four, two sets of five, light. Rest two to three minutes. Big breath, brace like you are landing a jump, sit between your hips, and drive up fast. This is the base of your attack position.

9. **ROMANIAN DEADLIFT (DB or BAR)**: A two. Romanian deadlift. Three sets of eight for the first two weeks, three sets of six in week three, two sets of six in the taper. Soft knees, push your hips back, keep the weight close and your back flat. This builds the hamstrings and lower back that hold you in position through whoops and braking bumps.

10. **BULGARIAN SPLIT SQUAT**: A three. Bulgarian split squat. Rear foot up on a bench, drop straight down, front knee stable. Eight per leg in week one, ten per leg in week two, heavier eights in week three, and six per leg to taper. Single leg strength is what saves you when you dab a foot or get kicked off line.

11. **FARMER CARRY (GRIP + ARM PUMP)**: A four. Farmer carry. Grab heavy dumbbells, crush the handles, stand tall, and walk forty yards. Three rounds in week one, four in week two, heavier fifty yard carries in week three, two to taper. Grip endurance is your first line of defense against arm pump.

12. **BALANCE: ATTACK POSITION HOLD**: A five. Balance, attack position hold. Stand on a pillow, a BOSU, or a balance board. Knees bent, elbows up, chin over the bars, holding a broomstick like handlebars. Thirty seconds, building to forty five. Have a buddy nudge you so you practice reacting and recovering.

13. **BALANCE: SINGLE-LEG RDL REACH**: A six. Single leg R D L reach. Stand on one leg, hinge forward, and reach for the floor while your back leg goes straight behind you. Keep the hips square and move slowly. This builds the ankle and hip stability you need on roots, rocks, and off-camber.

14. **RUN: ENGINE**: Wednesday. The engine run. Race-pace intervals. Thirty to forty minutes.

15. **WEDNESDAY INTERVALS**: Here are your intervals. Ten minute easy warm-up every time. Week one, six rounds of two minutes hard with two minutes easy. Week two, eight rounds. Week three, five rounds of three minutes hard. Week four, just four one minute efforts to stay sharp. Five minute cool-down.

16. **WHY INTERVALS WORK FOR OFF-ROAD**: Hard means eight out of ten. You can say two or three words, but not a full sentence. This copies what happens in a race: a hard push up a climb or to make a pass, then settling back in. Run hills or trails if you have them. If your knees are beat up, do the same intervals on a bike or rower. During recovery, keep moving. Don't stop and sit.

17. **STRENGTH B**: Strength B. Thursday. Upper body, pulling, core, and balance. About forty minutes.

18. **PULL-UPS (or LAT PULLDOWN)**: B one. Pull-ups. Full hang to chin over bar, pulling your elbows down to your ribs. Stop two reps before failure. Three sets in week one, four in week two, add a little weight for fives in week three, and two easy sets to taper. If you can't do a pull-up, use a band or a lat pulldown.

19. **DB BENCH PRESS or PUSH-UPS**: B two. Dumbbell bench press or push-ups. Shoulder blades pinched, lower slow, press fast. Tens for the first two weeks, heavier eights in week three, and two sets of eight to taper. This is the strength you use to muscle the bike out of ruts and pick it up after a get-off.

20. **ONE-ARM DB ROW**: B three. One-arm dumbbell row. Flat back, pull the weight to your hip, and pause at the top. Tens, then twelves, then heavier eights, then an easy taper week.

21. **GRIP: DEAD HANG + TOWEL HANG**: B four. Grip. Dead hang from the pull-up bar as long as you can. In week three, hang from two towels draped over the bar, which is much harder. Log your time and beat it. Stretch your forearms afterward.

22. **CORE CIRCUIT**: B five. Core circuit. Pallof press, ten per side. Dead bug, ten per side. Side plank, thirty seconds each side. Then a front plank for forty five seconds. Two rounds, then three, then three, then two to taper. Your core connects your legs to the bars. When it quits, your arms take over, and that is where pump starts.

23. **BALANCE: LATERAL BOUND + STICK**: B six. Lateral bound and stick. Jump sideways off one leg, land on the other, and stick it for a two count with a soft knee. Five per side, building to six. This trains fast reactions for dabs and saves.

24. **FRIDAY: EASY RUN + OPENERS**: Friday. Easy run, twenty to twenty five minutes at conversation pace. Then four strides of twenty seconds, fast but relaxed. Ten minutes of mobility for hips, hamstrings, and forearms. Hydrate all day, and get to bed early. Friday night's sleep matters most for Sunday's race.

25. **MONDAY: RECOVERY**: Monday is recovery. Thirty to forty minutes of easy bike, walking, or swimming. Foam roll your legs and lats. Stretch your forearms and wrists. Write down any tweaks from the race and adjust Tuesday's session if needed. Eat plenty of protein and carbs to rebuild.

26. **RACE-DAY WARM-UP  (15 MIN)**: Race day warm-up, fifteen minutes. Five minutes of easy jogging or jump rope. Leg swings and walking lunges. Three short ten second sprints to get your heart rate up, so the first lap isn't a shock. Then forearm squeezes and wrist circles. Finish ten to fifteen minutes before the start, and stay warm.

27. **FUEL + HYDRATION**: Fuel and hydration. Get about point eight to one gram of protein per pound of bodyweight every day. Eat a carb-heavy breakfast three hours before your start. Sip from your hydration pack every lap with electrolytes. For races over two hours, take in thirty to sixty grams of carbs per hour. Get protein and carbs within an hour after you finish.

28. **RULES FOR RACE SEASON**: Rules for race season. No heavy leg work within seventy two hours of a race. If you are sore or beat up, cut the session in half instead of skipping it. If you crashed hard, skip Strength A that week and focus on recovery. Sleep eight hours. And track your lifts and lap times, because what gets measured improves.

29. **SAFETY FIRST**: A quick safety note. This is general fitness information, not medical advice. Check with your doctor before starting any new program. If you feel sharp pain, dizziness, or chest pain, stop right away. Learn squat form with light weight first, and use a spotter or safety bars when you go heavy.

30. **SEE YOU AT THE FINISH**: That's the plan. Four weeks, built around your race weekend. Screenshot the weekly schedule, track your numbers, and I'll see you at the finish. Train smart, race hard.
